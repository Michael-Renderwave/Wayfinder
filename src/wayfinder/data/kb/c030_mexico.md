## Overview
One of the world's great travel countries: Maya and Aztec ruins, beach + cenote Yucatán, world-class food in CDMX and Oaxaca, colonial towns, and surf coasts from Baja to Oaxaca. Incredibly affordable, deeply layered, and easy to fly between hubs.

## Where to go
Top places: Mexico City, Cancún & Tulum, Oaxaca, Guanajuato, Guadalajara, Mérida, Los Cabos, Puerto Vallarta, Chichén Itzá, Chihuahua (Bacalar).

## Landmarks & sights
- Chichén Itzá (UNESCO) — the great Maya pyramid; arrive at opening for the "equinox shadow" acoustics; 2 hr from Cancún or Mérida
- Teotihuacan — the pyramids of the Sun & Moon, 40 min from CDMX; walk the 2 km Avenue of the Dead
- Palenque (Chiapas) — jungle Maya ruins, the Temple of the Inscriptions; go with a guide
- Tulum clifftop ruins — El Castillo over the turquoise Caribbean; best in the morning light
- Cenotes of the Yucatán — Dos Ojos, Gran Cenote, Suytun; swim, snorkel, or dive the sinkholes
- Frida Kahlo Museum "La Casa Azul" (CDMX) — the blue house in Coyoacán; book tickets online
- Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe (CDMX) — one of the most-visited Catholic shrines on Earth
- Xochimilco (CDMX) — floating flower canals on trajinera boats; Saturday afternoons
- Guanajuato — colorful canals & mule-cab rides, underground tunnels, the 1810 independence drama
- Tequila / Jalisco — agave fields, distillery tours, the blue agave heartland
- Los Cabos (Baja) — the "El Arco" arch at Land's End; whale shark season May–Sept
- Puerto Vallarta — the Malecón boardwalk, the Sierra Madre behind the town, Balandra Beach
- Bacalar (Quintana Roo) — the "Lake of Seven Colors," a lagoon with a sandbar & snorkel coves
- Casa de los Azulejos & the Zócalo (CDMX) — the tile-palace and the massive central plaza
- Mismaloya & Sayulita (Nayarit) — boho surf towns, artisan markets, quieter than PV
- Oaxaca's Monte Albán — Zapotec ruins on a flattened mountaintop above the city; sunset slot

## Food & drink
- tacos al pastor — spit-roasted pork with pineapple; CDMX's street stalls, Oaxaca's
- mole negro (Oaxaca) — 20+ ingredient chocolate-chile sauce; the state's signature dish
- pozole — hominy stew, regional (red/white/green); a Sunday tradition
- chilaquiles — crispy tortilla breakfast in salsa roja or verde, with crema & queso
- micheladas & elote — beer-lime-citrus cocktail and street corn with mayo/chili
- tlayudas (Oaxaca) — giant crispy tortilla pizza; go to any "tlayudería" in the center
- chapulines — toasted grasshoppers, a true Oaxacan snack; try them at a market
- cochinita pibil — sour-orchilla pork from Mérida/Yucatán; order in advance for feasts
- barbacoa (CDMX) — slow-cooked mutton or goat, Saturday mornings in the markets
- chiles en nogada — stuffed peppers in walnut cream, pomegranate garnish; Sept only
- pastela & gorditas (Guadalajara) — Jalisco street food; try the Elote "barbacoa" stands
- aguas frescas — fruit waters (horchata, jamaica, sandía); refill at any mercado
- mezcal flights — in Oaxaca's mezcalerías; ask for tobalá or espadín; sip, don't shot
- tamales — steamed corn dough with chile/cheese; breakfast in any city's market

## Things to do
- 2-day CDMX: Frida + Zócalo + Xochimilco + a food crawl through Roma & Condesa
- Yucatán loop: Chichén Itzá → cenote → Valladolid or Mérida → Uxmal
- Oaxaca food week: mole, mezcal, tlayudas, market day (Mardi in Tlacolula, Thu in Tlacolula)
- Guanajuato + Tequila: 1-day distillery + agave-field tour from the blue city
- Baja: whale-shark snorkel (La Paz, Jun–Sept) or scuba the "Sea of Cortez" (Galápagos of the sea)
- surf the Pacific: Puerto Escondido (Oaxaca), Sayulita (Nayarit), Los Cabos (Baja)
- colonial towns: San Cristóbal de las Casas, Cholula, Querétaro, Morelia
- wine country: Valle de Guadalupe (Baja) — 60+ boutique wineries, 3 hr from San Diego
- cenote dives: Dos Ojos, Sac Actun (Chichén area); book a PADI-certified operator
- hot springs: Cieneguita (Jalisco), El Tule (Oaxaca), Temozón (Yucatán)

## Where to stay

Stay options by city:
- CDMX: Casa Peláez (Roma, boutique), Four Seasons / St. Regis (Polanco, top), Albergue CDMX (Condesa, budget)
- Oaxaca: The House of the Muses (boutique), Grand Park by Posada Real (top), Hostal El Cielo (budget, center)
- Cancún: Le Blanc Spa Resort (top, all-in), Hard Rock (mid), Hostel X (budget, center)
- Tulum: Azulik (top, cliff-side), Habita Tulum (mid), Selina Tulum (budget, social)
- Guanajuato: Casa del Risco (boutique, 1800s mansion), Grand Park (mid), Albergue La Casa (budget)
- Mérida: Casa del Abuelo (boutique, center), Intercontinental (top, all-in), Selina Mérida (budget)
- Los Cabos: The Ritz-Carlton / St. Regis (top, beach), Hyatt Regency (mid), Hostel Cabos (budget, town)
- Puerto Vallarta: Las Vistinas (boutique, beachfront), Grand Palladium (top, all-in), Hostal PV (budget, center)

## Good to know
- Best: December–April (dry, warm). Summer (Jun–Sept) is hot & rainy on the Gulf; the Pacific is drier
- Learn a little Spanish — it goes a long way outside the resort zones
- US/Canadian visitors need a passport; the FMM entry form is online or at the airport
- Cash is king in markets & small towns; cards work in cities & resorts
- Tipping (propina) ~10–15% at sit-down restaurants; more for guides
- The Yucatán (CDMX, Mérida, Cancún, Oaxaca, PV) is a 1–2 hr flight apart — easy to combine
