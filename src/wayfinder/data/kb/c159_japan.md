## Overview
Japan: neon Tokyo by day, cherry-blossom Kyoto by night, the serenity of a Zen garden, the roar of a ramen shop at 2 a.m. — a country of beautiful contradictions. Compact rail, impeccable service, and some of the most concentrated cultural density on Earth.

## Where to go
Top places: Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Kanazawa, Nara, Hiroshima & Miyajima, Sapporo, Fukuoka, Hakone, Nikko, Takayama & Shirakawa-go.

## Landmarks & sights
- the Tokyo Skytree — 634 m tower in Sumida; observation deck at 350 m; best at dusk
- the Shibuya Scramble — the world's busiest pedestrian crossing; go at night for the neon
- the Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa) — Tokyo's oldest temple (645 AD); the Kaminarimon gate
- the Meiji Shrine (Shibuya) — 120,000 trees planted after the war; the forest path
- the Fushimi Inari Taisha (Kyoto) — 10,000+ vermilion torii gates up the mountain; go at dawn
- the Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion, Kyoto) — gold-leafed temple reflecting in its pond
- the Ryoan-ji rock garden (Kyoto) — 15 stones on raked gravel; the world's most famous Zen garden
- the Arashiyama Bamboo Grove (Kyoto) — 20 m bamboo canopy; 7 am to beat crowds
- the Nara Park deer — 1,200+ free-roaming sacred deer; bow to them for a "saisen" (prayer pellet)
- the Todai-ji Temple (Nara) — the world's largest wooden structure; the Great Buddha
- the Himeji Castle — the "White Heron" castle; Japan's best-preserved original keep (1609)
- the Matsumoto Castle — Japan's oldest original keep (1594); "Black Heron"
- the Hiroshima Peace Memorial — the Genbaku Dome + the Peace Park; deeply moving
- the Miyajima torii gate — the "floating" torii at high tide; sunset views from the back
- the Dogo Onsen (Matsue) — Japan's oldest hot spring (3,000+ years); the "Lord of the Rings" bath
- the Ginzan Onsen (Yamagata) — a row of 27 wooden ryokan in a snow-covered canyon; go in winter
- the Shirakawa-go — thatched-roof village (UNESCO); 250+ year-old farmhouses
- the Takayama Old Town — Edo-era merchant houses, morning food market, 20 min from Shirakawa-go
- the Hakone open-air museum — art + views of Mt. Fuji from the volcanic caldera
- the Mt. Fuji — best views from Lake Kawaguchi (Chureito Pagoda) or the 5th station
- the Sapporo Clock Tower — 1876 building; the heart of central Sapporo
- the Otaru Canal — gas lamps, glass workshops; 1 hr from Sapporo
- the Nishiki Market (Kyoto) — "Kyoto's kitchen"; 400+ shops; try takoyaki & pickles
- the Dotonbori (Osaka) — the neon food street; cranes, giant sign, takoyaki, kushikatsu
- the Osaka Castle — 16th-century keep (rebuilt 1931); the park + moat walk
- the Kinkaku-ji (Kyoto) — already listed above; the pond reflection is iconic

## Food & drink
- ramen — Tokyo's Ichiran (Shibuya), Kyoto's Nakama (Yasaka), Sapporo's Mitsudai (miso)
- sushi — Tokyo's Sushi Saito (omakase, reservation-only), Kyoto's Kitcho, Osaka's Nigiri no Kondo
- tempura — Tokyo's Tempura Kondo (Ginza), Kyoto's Kitcho (multi-course)
- wagyu beef — Osaka's Misono (Kuroge Wagyu), Kanazawa's Katsu Jiro (Kagoshima beef)
- okonomiyaki — Osaka's layered pancake; Misoji (Dotonbori)
- takoyaki — octopus balls; street carts in Osaka (Chin-Katsu), Tokyo (Nihonbashi)
- kushikatsu — Osaka's deep-fried skewers; Daruma (Dotonbori)
- unagi (eel) — Tokyo's Unagi Hachiro, Kyoto's Unagi Katsu
- mochi — rice cakes; Tokyo's Toriichi (Imari), Kyoto's Daimaru (Nishiki)
- wagashi (sweets) — Kyoto's Toraya (300-year-old confectionery shop)
- matcha — the best in Uji (Kyoto); try at a tea house in the Uji River area
- ramen at 2 a.m. — Tokyo's 24-hour shops (Ichiran 24h in Shibuya)
- the konbini culture — 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart; onigiri, ekiben, bento
- sake — Tokyo's Gekkeikan Sake Museum (free tasting), Kyoto's Nishikijin
- the izakaya (Japanese pub) — try in Osaka's Pontocho or Tokyo's Golden Gai (Shinjuku)
- the ekiben (train bento) — buy at a station kiosk; regional specialties
- the obanzai (Kyoto home cooking) — small plates of seasonal vegetables & tofu

## Things to do
- Tokyo: 3–4 day combo of Shibuya + Asakusa + Shinjuku (Golden Gai) + day trip to Hakone or Kamakura
- Kyoto: 2–3 days of temples + Arashiyama + Nara (half-day) + Fushimi Inari at dawn
- Osaka: 1–2 days of Dotonbori food + Osaka Castle + Shinsaibashi shopping + day trip to Nara
- Kanazawa: 1 day of the Old Town + Kenrokuen garden + the fish market (morning)
- the Japanese Alps: 2 days in Takayama + Shirakawa-go (day trip) + the Hida mountains
- the Ginzan Onsen: overnight ryokan (kaiseki dinner + breakfast included); 4 hr from Tokyo
- the Dogo Onsen: half-day in Matsue; the "Lord of the Rings" bath + the Onsen Museum
- the Sapporo beer fest (Aug) — the world's largest; try Sapporo Black & Golden
- the Otaru day trip from Sapporo — the Canal + the Glass Works + the Clock Tower
- the Mt. Fuji day trip from Tokyo — the Lake Kawaguchi route + the Chureito Pagoda
- the Nikko day trip — the Toshogu Shrine (shogun's mausoleum) + the Kegon Falls
- the Hiroshima + Miyajima day trip — the Peace Park + the floating torii gate
- the Sapporo Snow Festival (Feb) — 200+ ice & snow sculptures; 6 days
- the cherry blossom (sakura) season (late Mar–early Apr) — the Ueno Park, the Meguro River, the Philosopher's Path (Kyoto)
- the autumn leaves (koyo) season (Nov) — the Eikando Temple (Kyoto), the Ryoan-ji garden
- the Gion district (Kyoto) — see geiko & maiko at dusk; the Hanamikoji Street
- the Golden Gai (Shinjuku) — 200+ tiny bars in 6 narrow alleys; go at night
- the Tsukiji Outer Market (Tokyo) — the world's best fish market; go at 7 am for breakfast
- the Shinjuku Gyoen — 58 acres of formal gardens; free (400 yen)
- the Meiji Shrine forest walk — 40 min from Harajuku; the sacred forest path

## Good to know
- the JR Pass (7/14/21 day) — unlimited rail; buy before you arrive (not available in Japan since 2023 price hike)
- cash is still king — many small shops & temples are cash-only; carry 30,000–50,000 yen
- the 7-Eleven bento is genuinely great — try the salmon bento or the tempura bento
- no tipping — it's not expected and can be offensive
- the trains are punctual to the second — if they're late, they post an apology
- the "silent" Shinkansen — no phone calls, no loud conversations
- the ryokan etiquette — take off your shoes, use the onsen properly (wash first, then soak)
- the best time to visit: late Mar–early Apr (cherry blossoms) or Nov (autumn leaves)
- avoid the Golden Week (late Apr–early May) — domestic travel peaks, prices spike
- the Obon holiday (mid-Aug) — many people travel home; trains are packed
