## Overview
One of the world's most-visited countries for good reason: jungle temples, emerald islands, cheap incredible food, and a vibe that's bustling yet easy. From Bangkok's neon markets to the limestone karsts of Krabi and the hill towns of the north, there's something for every budget.

## Where to go
Top places: Bangkok, Chiang Mai & Chiang Rai, Phuket & Krabi, Koh Samui & Koh Phangan, Ayutthaya, Kanchanaburi, Khao Sok, Sukhothai, Pattaya.

## Landmarks & sights
- the Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew (Bangkok) — the Emerald Buddha; dress modestly, book ahead
- Wat Pho (Bangkok) — the 46 m reclining Buddha + the birthplace of Thai massage
- Wat Arun (Bangkok) — the "Temple of Dawn"; climb the prang for a sunset view
- Jim Thompson House (Bangkok) — a silk-tycoon's shophouse turned museum with moat gardens
- Doi Inthanon (Chiang Mai) — Thailand's highest peak (2,565 m); twin chedis
- Doi Suthep (Chiang Mai) — golden chedi up 306 steps; a panoramic city view
- Wat Rong Khun "White Temple" (Chiang Rai) — surreal white art temple; go early
- the Ayutthaya ruins — 400+ sandstone temple ruins; 1 hr from Bangkok
- Sukhothai Historical Park — the "cradle of Siam"; 200+ ruins, bike-friendly
- Khao Sok National Park — rainforest and limestone karsts; the "Jungle Cruise" lake
- Railay Beach & the limestone cliffs (Krabi) — you can climb them; boat in, road out
- the Phi Phi Islands — Maya Bay (from The Beach) and long-tail boat tours
- the Erawan "Seven Colours" Waterfalls (Kanchanaburi) — tiered emerald pools
- the Bridge over the River Kwai (Kanchanaburi) — WWII history + the railway museum
- Khao Yai National Park — Thailand's first park; elephants, langurs, waterfalls
- the Damnoen Saduak floating market (Ratchaburi) — canal food stalls, 1 hr from Bangkok

## Food & drink
- pad thai — the classic stir-fried noodles; best at street stalls in Bangkok & Chiang Mai
- tom yum — spicy-sour shrimp soup, the national dish; ask for "mai pet" (less spicy)
- tom kha kai — creamy coconut-chicken soup; milder, a great first bite
- green curry (gaeng keow wan) — Thai basil + coconut milk; order with rice
- som tam — papaya salad; Isaan-style (coarsely pounded) is the best
- moo ping — grilled pork skewers with sticky rice; ~40–60 baht a handful
- boat noodles (kuay teow reua) — tiny-bowl spicy beef-noodle soup; a Bangkok icon
- mango sticky rice (khao niao mamuang) — the ultimate dessert; find ripe yellow mangoes
- khao gai — Thai "chicken rice" (Hainanese); clear-souped or dry
- roti (roti Thai) — thin Indian-style flatbread with egg & sugar; breakfast in the south
- khanom krok — coconut rice cakes; the 20-baht snack everywhere
- iced tea (cha yen) & Thai milk tea — sweet, condensed-milk orange; a daily ritual
- khao po thong — "golden" glutinous rice with durian or mango; a Bangkok dessert
- gai yang — grilled chicken with peanut sauce & rice; a Chiang Mai staple

## Things to do
- 2-day Bangkok: Grand Palace + Wat Pho + Wat Arun + a floating market + a rooftop dinner
- Chiang Mai hill loop: Doi Suthep → an ethical (no-riding) elephant sanctuary → the night bazaar
- Krabi island-hopping: Railay → Ao Nang → a 2-day long-tail boat to the Four Islands
- Koh Samui + Phangan: a few days of surf & diving, one night on the full-moon party island
- the north: Chiang Rai White Temple → the hill-tribe villages
- Khao Sok: two days of jungle + the "Jungle Cruise" lake
- scuba & diving: the Similan Islands (Nov–May, the best dive season)
- Thai massage & spa — a 1-hr session is 300–600 baht; the local price is fair
- the "Bangkok → Chiang Mai → Krabi → Samui" 14-day loop — a classic route
- the Isaan food crawl: som tam, moo ping, khao man gai, khao gai in Nakhon Ratchasima
- Khao Yai day trip: waterfalls + an ethical elephant feeding

## Where to stay

Stay options by city:
- Bangkok: Aman Bangkok / The Okura Prestige (top, riverside), Mandarin Oriental (top), Lub d Bangkok Siam (budget, social)
- Chiang Mai: The 8 (boutique, Old Town), Anantara Chiang Mai (mid), Prestige Hostel (budget, Old Town)
- Phuket: Amanpuri (top, beachfront), Hyatt Regency (mid, Patong), Lub d Phuket Patong (budget)
- Krabi: Rayavadee (top, Railay), Hyatt Regency Krabi (mid), The 8 Krabi (budget, Ao Nang)
- Koh Samui: The Siam / Alaia (top, beachfront), Hyatt Regency (mid, Chaweng), Lub d Samui (budget, Bophut)
- Ayutthaya: Amari Ayutthaya (top, center), Ratchaprasong (mid), a local guesthouse (budget)

## Good to know
- Best: November–February (cool, dry). March–May is hot (35°C+). June–October is rainy but the islands are still great
- Learn a few Thai words — "sawasdee" (hello), "khop khun" (thank you), "arai" (how much)
- Dress modestly at temples — cover shoulders & knees; shoes off
- Agree on a taxi/meter price before you go — the "farang price" is real
- Tipping ~10% is polite at sit-down restaurants; not expected at street stalls
- The Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) is now filled online within 72 hr of arrival
- Cash is king in the north & islands; cards work in Bangkok & the big cities
- "Farang" is the local word for a foreigner — a label, not an insult
